    How to use the map

    • Move around: drag the map. Scroll, pinch or use + − to zoom.
    • Turn the city: ⟲ ⟳ (or the [ ] keys) show it from all four sides.
    • Find a place: search any street, address or landmark at the top.
    • Buses and planes: hover or tap one to see its route or flight.
    • ★ lists places worth a look, each with a short note. Tap a pin on the map to read one.
    • ◐ switches time of day, weather, names, cars, buses and planes.
    • Share: the arrow copies a link to exactly this view; the camera saves a picture of it.

    What you're looking at

    Every block of Burlington, South Burlington and Winooski was redrawn in a city-builder style by an open-source AI image model, working from Google's 3D map of the city. It's an illustration: small details are invented, and some won't match what's really there. Street and place names are drawn on top from OpenStreetMap, so they stay sharp when you zoom in. How it was made, and what's wrong with it.

    The sky follows Burlington's real sunset and the current weather at BTV. Buses move along their routes on GMT's published timetable, not live GPS. Planes are live positions from ADS-B receivers.

    The cars are simulated, not tracked: they drive OpenStreetMap's streets and stop at its traffic lights, and there are more or fewer of them by the hour, the way a typical day goes. At night the street lamps and lit windows are placed to suit each street and building, not surveyed; the runway and breakwater lights follow OpenStreetMap.
